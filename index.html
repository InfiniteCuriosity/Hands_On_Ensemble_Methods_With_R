1 This book will put the most accurate tools to model nature in your hands

This book will guide you through the entire process of building your own ensemble models from beginning to end. I’ve done my very best to make the book very interesting, fun, and practical. There are lots of examples using real world data with all the steps included.

Nature is most accurately described, modeled and predicted using a diverse set of individual models and ensembles of models. Ensembles are the most accurate description of nature we have up to this time. Ensembles are the current best understanding of nature. This book will give you the tools and skills to build a diverse range of individual models and ensembles of models for numeric, classification, logistic and time series data.

A Teacher will show you how to do things. A Mentor challenges you to grow and succeed. And a Counselor is committed to guiding you to do this process in the healthiest possible way. My goal is to be all three for you in this book, based on a lifetime of very successful experience. Welcome to the journey!

This book will be an attempt to answer the single most important question in all of science: How can nature be modeled in the most accurate way, given what we know at this time? The goal is to tell the truth in the evidence and make the most accurate predictions possible. This will be done by modeling, and you will learn about the process in this book.

The goal is to help you become your own modeler, and help you produce the best results from any given data set.

1.1 Features of this book to help you get the most out of your experience

1.1.1 Each chapter will include a preview (like this), full text, and a review. I call the system delayed repetition, and it’s the method that helps me learn the fastest.

I’ve had the pleasure to learn a very broad range of skills in my life. For example, I was the only bilingual staff person in an office for quite a while. I’ve also studied extreme sports, several styles of music, led two not for profit organizations (local chapter of Amnesty International and the Chicago Apple User Group), and much more. One of the most important skills I’ve learned is how I learned well. Certain methods are more successful for me, others are less successful. The most successful method I have found is a system I called delayed repetition. I learn faster when I see a preview of something, then do it, then do a review. I might do that over again, but that delayed repetition of working on a part of the process multiple times helps me learn much faster than if I just try to see it once and then apply it.

Another benefit I have found is that delayed repetition results in longer retention for me. I have found that if I slow down my learning process and include delayed repetition, that my retention and accuracy of skills are much better than if I do not use delayed repetition.

An example I have always enjoyed happened when I was a student in music school at Western Michigan University. My private violin instructor encouraged me to memorize a piece before I started practicing it. I told him that I found that confusing and was not sure what he was asking. He clarified that he wanted me to know the notes of the piece first, and then we will work out, over and over again, various ways to interpret it, so that the final result would be my choice of a range of interpretations, not his interpretation. This also resulted in me learning the music much faster than I had previous selections I worked out while I was a student in music school.

This book will reflect that understanding that delayed repetition is a fast way to learn and retain skills. Every section will start with a preview, followed by the main body of the text, followed by a summary. There will also be practice exercises and other ways to review. My experience has shown, over and over and over again, that it is the delayed repetition where I learn the most, and this book will reflect that method, and hopefully demonstrate the success I have had learning in so many different areas. I found delayed repetition to be extremely successful in virtually every area I have applied, and I’m thrilled to bring delayed repetition to you in this book.

In my experience, delayed repetition is a much faster to learn than any other system I’ve ever found. Since delayed repetition works in so many different areas, I encourage you to feel free to experiment with it in other areas in addition to learning data science and building ensembles.

1.1.4 The process of discovery

What I see frequently described as a scientific method, or the scientific process, is only the second part of the actual scientific process. The part that I see involves testing, verification, and similar aspects of the process. What I do not see very often is the first part of the process, which is the process of discovery. Many times, great discoveries are made, then tested and verified. It is extremely rare that anything is discovered and verified at the same time. Therefore, an extremely valuable skill in the scientific method is the process of discovery. This book will encourage you to do that, and each section will have a part devoted to discovery.

Discovery can be greatly enhanced by seeing connections and relationships that other people frequently miss. This book will present various data science methods to help you see connections in relationships that are often overlooked. These will help you come up with more accurate descriptions and predictions on new data.

I personally find the process of discovery to be very fun and exciting, and I will do my best to share my enthusiasm for with you in each section.

1.2.5 Repeated random resampling. You’ll get more accurate answers when you randomly resample.

One of my personal frustrations when I see data science reports and presentations is when the authors only run a dead at a single time. Inevitably, if you randomly resample the data and then rerun the analysis, you will get a different result. We will use that observation to our advantage. We will randomly resemble in every report, and then provide the results from that random sampling. This will give us a more accurate result in the real world, then just running the analysis a single time.

1.2.7 Include confidence intervals for all results, not just a specific value (do not say, “the model with the best RMSE had a result of X”).

We will also include 95% confidence intervals in our results. This will make it much more relatable to your reader, and give you a more accurate answer.

For example, if the recommendation is that the housing price is $200,000, that is not nearly as accurate as saying that there’s a 95% degree of confidence at the housing price will be in the range of: $165,000 - $235,000.

And building on the previous point, we are going to use multiple models and see the 95% confidence interval for each of these models. So if another model had a confidence interval of: $190,000 - $210,000, that would be something to share with the customer, that the second model has a narrow result than the first. Will actually be able to do this with all 32 models so we can see how accurate each of them actually are after we have re-sampled them multiple times.

1.2.8 Test for overfitting, only use models that do not overfit on new data

Overfitting is a flaw that can happen in a mathematical analysis. Overfitting happens when the model corresponds too closely to the training data, but fails to correspond on the testing data or on new data. Overfitting is a value which can be measured and tested, and the results can be ranked from lowest to highest (when applied to multiple models). We will look at how to measure and test for overfitting for each of the models, and report the results that so that we use the model which has the best accuracy and the lowest over fitting value.

We will look at how to plot overfitting for each resample, for both the train value and the holdout (test) values. As this graph shows, lower values are more accurate, and it’s clear some models are more accurate than others. This is a plot with free scales (y-values), but it’s still clear to see how each of the 32 models performed with the Insurance data set.

Higher values are better, most researchers reject the results if they are below a pre-specified value (such as 0.05 or 0.10). 1.15 Outliers and Separators that provoke questions, not answers.

Study what separates top performers that no one else is doing

Don’t just study what the top performers are doing. The best information will come when we know what the top performers are doing that no one else is doing. Don’t just study what the top performers are doing, study what separates top performers from everyone else.

In data science, the top performers are using ensembles, but very few others are using ensembles. So we will learn how to build ensembles of models, because that is the biggest separator between top performing statistical models and the rest.

We’ll also see how to do separate top performers from everything else in our analysis, graphics, and recommendations. It’s not only what people are doing, it’s the values and commitments and disciplines that strongly contribute to all the difference, we will look at those in this book.

What data scientists actually do

What separates data science from all other fields is we build models to make predictions and test everything. The function of data science is to test everything in a way that is neutral, fair, and reproducible. We share the results of the tests.

In this book we will test everything from model accuracy to overfitting to how similar the predicted distribution is to the actual data, and much, much more.

We will see the same test applied to a range of models, so the test results can be ranked.
